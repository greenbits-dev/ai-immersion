# Lesson 1 — The big picture

*Session 1. Read time: ~10 minutes.*

There are three ways to work with Claude. Same brain, three different bodies.

## Layer 1: Claude chat (claude.ai)

A conversation in the browser. You ask, it answers. It can search the web, read files you upload, write documents.

- **What it's for**: thinking, writing, learning, one-off questions.
- **Limit**: everything disappears when the chat ends. It can't touch your computer. You copy-paste results out by hand.

## Layer 2: Cowork (Claude on your files)

Claude gets access to a folder of your files. It can read your documents, organize them, produce new ones — without you copy-pasting.

- **What it's for**: real work on real documents — reports, research, spreadsheets, filing.
- **Limit**: it works *on files*, not *on systems*. It doesn't build software, run programs, or publish anything.

## Layer 3: Claude Code (the terminal)

Claude gets hands. It runs in your terminal, inside a project folder, and can read files, write files, run programs, install tools, use git, and deploy to the internet. You describe what you want; it does the work and shows you the result.

- **What it's for**: building things — websites, tools, automations — and having an assistant that operates your computer.
- **Limit**: with hands comes responsibility. This is why most of this course is about *how to steer it safely*, not about typing commands.

> **The one-sentence version**: chat answers questions, Cowork works on your documents, Claude Code builds and operates things. This course takes you to layer 3.

## The AI competence ladder — where you are, where this course takes you

AI skill isn't one thing you have or don't. It's a ladder, and almost everyone is on the bottom rungs. Here is the ladder in plain terms (percentages are rough shares of professionals today):

| Level | Name | One line | ~Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unaware | Still doing everything manually; hasn't really tried AI | 10–20% |
| 1 | Aware | Has tried ChatGPT/Claude; occasional, not part of how they work | 15–25% |
| 2 | Assisted worker | Uses AI daily to draft, summarize, think; real hours saved weekly | 25–35% |
| 3 | Enabled professional | AI is embedded: reusable prompts, delegates multi-step tasks, uses agent tools — **builds real things with Claude Code** | 12–20% |
| 4 | Workflow integrator | Connects AI to real systems (calendar, email, databases, APIs); ships working products to the internet | 6–12% |
| 5 | System builder | Designs systems of multiple coordinated AI agents with checkpoints and error handling | 2–5% |
| 6–9 | Architect → frontier | Production platforms, AI products with real users, infrastructure others build on, defining how the field works | <2% combined |

Three things to take from this:

1. **You are probably at 0–2.** So is almost everyone — including most people whose job is supposedly "tech". No catching up to do; the ladder is young.
2. **This course targets Level 3, with a real glimpse of 4.** Daily AI use with structure, delegating multi-step work to an agent, and by session 4 something Level-4-shaped: a real product (your site) deployed to the internet. That already puts you ahead of roughly 80% of professionals.
3. **The floor keeps rising.** What needed a senior engineer in 2024 is a Level 3 skill with 2026 tools. Climbing one level per year is a completely realistic pace — and each level compounds, because the systems you build keep working for you.

Two honest notes: the ladder has two valid tracks — using AI inside your existing job (analyst, marketer, manager) and building things with AI. This course teaches the **builder** track, but everything here transfers directly to the professional track. And the level is not a badge; it's a diagnosis. Re-read the table after session 4 and place yourself again.
