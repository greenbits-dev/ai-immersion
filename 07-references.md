# Lesson 7 — References: who to follow, what to watch

*A deliberately short list. Five good sources beat fifty bookmarks.*

## People to follow

- **Andrej Karpathy** — the best explainer of what LLMs actually are. Start with his YouTube "Intro to Large Language Models"; his "Software 3.0" talk frames why plain English is becoming a programming language.
- **Matt Pocock** ([aihero.dev](https://aihero.dev)) — practical AI-assisted development; source of the "dumb zone" framing from Lesson 2.
- **Simon Willison** ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net)) — the most grounded ongoing commentary on what these tools can and can't do. No hype.
- **Anthropic's engineering blog** — "Claude Code: Best practices for agentic coding" is the canonical guide once you're up and running.

## Docs worth actually reading

- **Claude Code documentation** — the "Getting started" section before session 2, the rest as needed.
- **GitHub's "Hello World" guide** — the gentlest introduction to repos, commits, and branches.

## Watch

- Karpathy — **"Intro to Large Language Models"** (~1h). The single best foundation; watch before or right after session 1.
- Karpathy — **"Software 3.0"** talk. Why this course exists.
- Any recent "Claude Code tutorial" under 30 minutes, for seeing the loop live before you run it yourself.

## How to keep learning after session 5

1. **Ship weekly.** One small finished improvement to your site per week beats a course binge.
2. **Ask Claude itself.** "Explain MCP like I'm new" inside Claude Code is better than most blog posts — it can demo the answer.
3. **Steal patterns, not code.** When you see someone's workflow (a CLAUDE.md trick, a skill, a checklist), copy the *pattern* into your own projects.
4. **Re-take the ladder test.** Re-read the competence ladder in [Lesson 1](01-big-picture.md) every few months and honestly re-place yourself.
5. **Once something runs on its own, check that it still runs.** The first time you schedule a job (a nightly backup, a weekly report), keep a list of everything that is supposed to run. A job that quietly stops leaves no error behind. [Silent When Healthy](https://www.senye.me/principles/silent-when-healthy) explains the habit and links a free checker.
